This was my first trip to the George, on a very rainy Tuesday night. I had already scoped out the exterior on Google maps looking for somewhere to lock my bike (there isn't anywhere, I ended up walking up there anyway) but I was still struck by how big it was inside. The heavy oak-beamed ceiling is high and there's plenty of space forming an L-shape around the bar. This in turn is surrounded by booth tables, with the upholstery much the worse for wear. It may have seemed all the larger for how empty it was, with a solitary bloke propping up the bar and one table occupied with a few mid thirties folk. The Courage Best on cask looked very inviting, but unfortunately had just been moved and hadn't had time to settle, meaning I had to settle for John Smiths. It was served in a clean glass and went down smoothly so I wasn't too put out.

The dartboard was down a small set of stairs in a room at the back of the pub, just past the Christmas decorations, apparently bigger than last years. The board was unbranded, but recognisable as a Winmau Blade 6 (confirmed by the Landlady, although she was evasive about her source for the board). I've seen these at the [Sandringham](https://fredcook.co.uk/2026/04/04/The-Sandringham), maybe it's a special pub tie-in to allow for board rotation not being given away by the logos at the top and bottom. The board was in great condition, with minimal signs of wear. The darts landed with lovely gentle thuds with barely a bounce-out to mention. There was a mount for a second dart board across a presumably un-used door, however, the board itself had been taken out of commission. When we arrived the board was being used by a couple using the pub's darts, relegating us to a game of pool. The only thing noteworthy about the game was that it only cost 50p.

The oche was a wooden baton, roughly a centimetre high. Slightly too low to really slam your foot against, but navigable without looking down, and perhaps less of a trip hazard than other raised oches. There's a small patch of worn away carpet in the centre of the oche showing how much use its had. Although this isn't out of keeping with the rest of the pub, which has definitely seen better days. I didn't measure the oche but it felt right, I trust that it was done properly in such a darts loving pub. There was plenty of space to [slide up and down the oche](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/viyOhc18Dxc) depending on your style and the lay of the darts.

Scoring options were two large chalkboards either side of the board, a standard set-up but tried and tested. There was definitely space to have a scorer unless someone is throwing particularly wildly. The secondary set-up in the room (with board currently missing) also had a small chalkboard, visible in the wide shot below. You can also see some trophies above the dartboard cabinet, hinting at the George's illustrious past (and possibly present).

We only got a few games in, due to the board being occupied when we arrived and last orders being called around 9pm due to lack of custom. As we left I got the chance to ask the Landlady about the provenance of the unbranded Winmau Blade 6, mentioned above. She also mentioned there was a snazzy [Super League](https://westbristoldarts.leaguerepublic.com/fg/1_62727327.html) set-up upstairs, where the restrictions on board and lighting are much tighter for an official game. All in all I enjoyed playing at the Knowle, it's always a treat to play on a board with dedicated space, rather than being an after thought or aesthetic choice. I will have to come back another time, hopefully when the atmosphere is a bit livelier and possibly to spectate a Super League match to see the standard.
